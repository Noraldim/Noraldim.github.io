Land Segmentation and Smart-City Planning from Satellite Imagery

Read the paperDownload PDFSource code

What it is about

Every new district starts with the same question: where is there land that can be built on? Today that is still answered by hand, from cadastral records and photo-interpretation. Urban-Planner answers it end-to-end. It segments open land in 10 m Sentinel-2 imagery with deep encoder–decoder networks, then hands the mask to a rule-based planner that places a full district (houses, apartments, shops, schools, a hospital, a stadium) with no overlaps.

What the paper covers

  • Turning the 2,433 patches of the PASTIS benchmark into a binary open land vs. background target (60.1% positive pixels), and analysing the resulting dataset.
  • Seven models compared under one metric suite: U-Net with ResNet-34, ResNet-101 and VGG-16 encoders, and DeepLabV3+ with ResNet-50, ResNet-101, Xception and EfficientNet-B5. Each one is described down to the layer its features are tapped from.
  • The result: DeepLabV3+ beats U-Net and is more robust to configuration, and ResNet-101 is the best encoder in both families. The layer-level analysis shows that the Xception and EfficientNet-B5 taps sit one stride level higher than intended.
  • An honest reading of the protocol. The reported scores are computed on images seen during training, so they are upper bounds, and the paper proposes a fold-based protocol to fix that.
  • A formal, reproducible version of the smart-city planner. When a tile fails, it is because the land is too fragmented, not because there is too little of it.
