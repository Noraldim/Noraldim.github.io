TÜBİTAK 2209-A research project · 2025 – 2026 · 4 pages · Turkish

AI-Assisted Plant Disease Detection and Decision Support Platform

Read the paperDownload PDF

What it is about

Plant diseases are usually noticed too late, and checking large fields by eye or in a lab is slow and expensive. This TÜBİTAK 2209-A project, supervised by Dr. Ayşenur Türkmen at Bilecik Şeyh Edebali University, builds a fully software-based platform that works on continuous video instead of single photos. It finds each leaf, names the disease, records it once, and turns the result into treatment advice. It works with any drone, field camera or Raspberry Pi, and any trained model can be swapped in while it runs.

What the paper covers

  • An API-first architecture on Python, FastAPI, Ultralytics, PyTorch, OpenCV and SQLite. The web interface, a future mobile app and edge devices all share one REST interface, and inference never runs inside a request handler.
  • Source abstraction: screen capture (for a drone app mirrored to a PC), video files, USB/CSI cameras and RTSP streams can be switched at runtime without stopping the pipeline.
  • Decoupled capture and inference through a single latest-frame slot instead of a queue. Old frames are dropped, so latency never builds up and the system adapts itself to the hardware it runs on.
  • Two-stage detection: YOLO (v8/11) locates each leaf and ByteTrack gives it an ID, then a MobileNetV2 or ViT classifier names the disease on the crop.
  • De-duplication: one diseased leaf in view for 30 s at 30 FPS would create about 900 identical records. Persistent track IDs, a per-ID cool-down, a confidence threshold and a minimum box area cut that to one.
  • A decision-support engine that maps each disease to its risk, symptoms, treatment and a natural remedy. A model registry hot-swaps any uploaded .pt model, with a staged path from ready-made models to fully custom ones.
  • Raspberry Pi edge node in two modes: stream video to a GPU server, or run a lightweight YOLO11n on the Pi and send only detections, which saves bandwidth.
  • An honest baseline (Phase 0): the untrained system identified the plant species correctly but forced out-of-scope plants into the nearest disease class with up to 0.91 confidence. High confidence is not correctness, and this finding justifies training on field data with an “unknown” class.
