ASTRON: Real-Time UAV Detection and Trajectory Prediction with YOLOv8 and a Kalman Filter

Read the paperDownload PDFSource code

What it is about

Detecting a drone in a video tells you where it is. Predicting where it will be is what lets a camera, a gimbal or an interceptor follow it. ASTRON is a compact real-time system that couples a YOLOv8 detector with a discrete-time Kalman filter built on OpenCV. The paper is a complete, self-contained account of it, from the network architecture to the filter maths to simulations that drive the actual OpenCV filter.

What the paper covers

  • The YOLOv8 architecture, and a single-class uav detector trained for 100 epochs on the IHA Nesne Tespit dataset: precision 0.927, recall 0.905, mAP@0.5:0.95 of 0.582.
  • The Kalman filter derived from first principles and specialised to ASTRON’s four-state constant-velocity model.
  • What every implementation choice really does: OpenCV’s default covariances (Q = R = I, P0 = 0), the correct-then-predict call order that turns the output into a one-step-ahead forecast, and the coupling with a colour-threshold localiser.
  • A proof that the filter settles into a steady-state α–β tracker with α = 0.822 and β = 0.422.
  • Reproducible experiments on straight-line motion, a circular manoeuvre and missed detections. The default tuning amplifies detector noise, and a noise ratio r/q ≈ 100 fixes it. When the target is missed, the localiser reports the image origin, which can drag the prediction by more than 500 px. A simple gate cuts that to 11 px.
  • A concrete roadmap from ASTRON to a full tracking-by-detection system.
